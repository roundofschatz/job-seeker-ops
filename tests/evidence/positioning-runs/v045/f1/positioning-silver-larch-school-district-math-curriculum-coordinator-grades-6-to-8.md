## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Nadia reads a test result as next week's lesson plan. At Cottonwood Springs she put her whole math department on the same unit tests, met with them every week to regroup students by their unit test results, and kept at it until her eighth graders' results against the state standard climbed. We need that habit now. Our eighth graders have slipped against that same standard, and a new curriculum lands in all four of our middle schools this year. A change that size is when students fall behind before anyone sees it. Her weekly data meetings exist to see it.

Our concern was reach. She has run one school's math department. This job asks her to lead teachers in four schools she doesn't run, through an adoption she has never led. What answered it was the monthly training she led for math teachers from three Basalt Creek middle schools, on reading unit test results and planning reteach lessons. She also sat on her district's committee for a textbook adoption and tried two of the finalists in her own classes, so she has watched candidate textbooks at work with real students.

We'd hire her for the way she turns results into the next lesson for the students who need it. We'd expect her first months to go into learning our four schools and their teachers, and into building common unit tests for the new curriculum.
