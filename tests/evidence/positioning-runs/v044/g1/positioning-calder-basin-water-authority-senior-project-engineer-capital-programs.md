## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Graham plans a grant project backward from the day the money would be lost. At Harlow Valley he kept a bid calendar that started from each grant deadline, met the state's funding officer every month, and got every one of his state revolving fund projects out to bid on schedule without losing any grant money. Our water main program needs that habit now. If our projects miss the bid date, the state takes the money back, and the board's new capital plan means this deadline won't be the last one.

Our concern was size and time. Our program is bigger than the one he ran, and our deadline is closer. What answered it was how much of this work he already does. He manages design consultants, reviews plans for a steady stream of water main projects every year, and has run bidding on a long list of construction contracts. He also started the sewer lining program at the same utility, so he has built a way of working from the ground up as well as run one.

We'd hire him to get our grant projects out to bid on time. We'd expect his first months to go into learning our consultants, our project list and the state's schedule.

4. **What the reader should believe:** Graham gets grant-funded water projects to bid before the grant deadline without losing the money, and Calder Basin's water main program has to do exactly this by June 2027.
