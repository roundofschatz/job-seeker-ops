## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Nadia treats a unit test as the plan for next week's teaching. At Cottonwood Springs she had her department give common unit tests, meet every week over the results and regroup students by them. Her eighth graders' scores on the state test climbed while she ran the department that way. Our own eighth graders are slipping on that test.

Our new curriculum will need teachers in every middle school working from shared assessments. She has already taught that routine beyond her own building. She led monthly sessions where math teachers from several of her district's middle schools worked on reading unit test results and planning reteach lessons. She also designed a summer math program for incoming sixth graders who had scored below grade level, so she has caught students before they fell further behind.

Our concern was scale. She has chaired one department and sat on an adoption committee, and she hasn't led an adoption across several schools. Her district sessions answered most of it, because they show teachers from other schools taking up her routine. She also piloted two finalist textbooks in her own classes, so she has taught from new materials in the middle of an adoption.

We'd hire her to make data meetings a weekly habit in every middle school, and we'd expect her first months to go into getting to know our teachers and leading her first adoption.

4. **What the reader should believe:** Nadia raised 8th grade math results by getting teachers to act on common unit test results every week, and she can lead Silver Larch's middle school math teachers in that habit through the new curriculum.
