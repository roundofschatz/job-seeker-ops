## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Nadia uses a unit test to decide what her teachers do next week. As chair she moved her department onto common unit tests and weekly meetings where teachers regroup students by what the tests show. She led with her students' results, and when we asked how, she described what her teachers did with the evidence.

Our schools need that habit this year. Our eighth graders have been losing ground on the state math standard, and our middle schools are starting a new math curriculum. The curriculum will give our teachers new lessons, and she'd give them a weekly way to see which students those lessons are missing. Her summer program for incoming sixth graders who had scored below grade level shows she goes after those students early.

Our concern was reach. She's run one school's math department, and this job means leading teachers in all our middle schools through an adoption. What answered it was the work she's done past her own building. She led monthly sessions on reading unit test results for math teachers from several of her district's middle schools, and she sat on its committee for a textbook adoption and piloted finalists in her own classes.

We'd hire her for the way she gets teachers to act on what their students' tests show. We'd expect her first months to go into learning our schools and the new curriculum.

NADIA HADDAD

Dear Silver Larch School District hiring team,

Last spring 38% of Silver Larch's 8th graders met the state math standard, down from 46% in 2023, and this year your four middle schools start a new math curriculum. At Cottonwood Springs Middle School, where I chair the math department, 61% of our 8th graders met that same standard in 2024, up from 44% in 2021. Your new curriculum will give your teachers new lessons, and common unit assessments can show them which students those lessons miss before they fall behind.

In 2019 I became department chair and started moving our department toward common unit tests, so every math teacher in the department would look at the same evidence. We made the switch in fall 2021. That winter our teachers started meeting every week to go over the results, and every three weeks they regrouped students by the latest unit tests.

From 2022 to 2024 I also led monthly professional development for 18 math teachers from three middle schools in our district. Each of those Basalt Creek sessions covered how to read unit test results and how to plan reteach lessons from them. Your 30 math teachers would get sessions like those, and I'd coach your new math teachers in their own classrooms on reading their students' unit tests and planning the reteach lessons that follow.

When Basalt Creek chose a new 6th grade math textbook in 2020, I sat on the district's math curriculum review committee. As chair in 2020 I still taught my own classes. Before the choice, I piloted two of the three finalist books in those classes. For your new curriculum, I'd build its common unit assessments with your teachers, and then teachers in all four schools could regroup students by those results, the way ours do at Cottonwood Springs.

I raise middle school math results by getting teachers to act on common unit tests, and I can lead Silver Larch's math teachers in using them through the new curriculum. Could we meet to look at the first 8th grade unit in your new curriculum and the common test I'd build for it with your teachers?

Best,
Nadia Haddad
