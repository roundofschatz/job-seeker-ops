## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Graham gets the groundwork done before the work reaches the street. At Harlow Valley he started the lining program, wrote its specifications and ran a meeting for the residents before each street was lined, and his lining drew far fewer complaints per mile than open-trench work did. He runs his grant projects the same way, from a bid calendar that works back from each grant deadline. Our new lining group needs that habit, because we've hired out all of our lining until now, and whoever sets the group up will decide how we specify the work and how we treat the streets it runs through.

Our concern was how recent his lining work is. It comes from his years as a project engineer, and since he moved into his current job, his record has been about getting grant-funded projects to bid. What answered it was that Harlow Valley still uses the lining specifications he wrote, and his current job still covers sewer projects as well as water. He also lined more sewer main there than our first target.

We'd hire him to set up the lining group and lead its first projects, and we'd expect his first months to go into learning our sewer system and the contractors who've done our lining so far.

```text
Dear Calder Basin Water Authority hiring team,

Calder Basin's new trenchless lining group has its first ten miles of sewer main to line, and until now outside contractors have done all of the authority's sewer lining. Dana Ruiz, who I used to work with, is in your engineering group and said she'd pass my name along. Whoever sets up the group will decide how Calder Basin specifies its lining and how it treats the streets the work runs through. I started a trenchless sewer lining program at Harlow Valley Utilities in 2017, when I was a project engineer there.

I wrote the program's specifications for cured-in-place pipe lining. Harlow Valley still uses them. I ran the meetings for residents before each street was lined. By 2020 the program had lined 14 miles of main at about 40% of the cost of open-trench replacement. A lined mile drew about a quarter of the complaints that a mile of open-trench work did.

I'm open to either program, including the water main program and its deadline to get $40 million in projects to bid by June 2027. Each of Harlow Valley's state revolving fund grants came with its own deadline, so I keep a bid calendar that works back from each one and meet the state's funding officer every month. From 2021 to 2025 I took nine grant-funded projects to bid, $31 million in all. All nine reached bid on schedule, and Harlow Valley kept every dollar of its grant money.

My first months as your Senior Project Engineer would go into learning Calder Basin's sewer system and the contractors who've done its lining so far. Your board approved a ten-year, $600 million capital plan in May, so the bidding won't stop when the June 2027 deadline passes. Getting projects to bid on schedule is my job at Harlow Valley now, and I'd bring that to whichever program I lead.

I've set up a sewer lining program and lined more sewer main than your first target, and I can do the same for Calder Basin's new lining group. Could we talk about how the group will line its first ten miles, with its own crews or with outside contractors?

Best,
Graham Whitfield
```
