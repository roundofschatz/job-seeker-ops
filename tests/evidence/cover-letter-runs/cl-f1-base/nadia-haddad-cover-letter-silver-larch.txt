Dear Hiring Committee,

Between 2021 and 2024, the share of 8th graders at Cottonwood Springs Middle School who met the state math standard rose from 44% to 61%. I've chaired the school's math department since 2019, after six years teaching 7th and 8th grade math there, and I'm applying for your Math Curriculum Coordinator position for grades 6 to 8.

That growth came from work our nine math teachers did together. I built common unit tests for grades 6 to 8, and we moved to them in fall 2021. That winter we started weekly data meetings, where teachers look at what the tests show and regroup the students who need a lesson again.

Your four middle schools adopt a new math curriculum this year. The new curriculum will give your teachers new lessons, and common unit assessments will show them which students those lessons are missing. That's the work I'd lead with your 30 math teachers.

I've led this work beyond my own school. From 2022 to 2024 I ran monthly professional development for 18 math teachers from three Basalt Creek middle schools, on reading unit test results and planning reteach lessons. I served on Basalt Creek's math curriculum review committee for its 2020 adoption of a new 6th grade textbook, and I piloted two of the three finalists in my classes.

I've also worked on catching students before they fall behind. In 2023 I designed and ran a four-week summer math program for 120 incoming 6th graders who had scored below grade level in 5th grade. That fall, 52% of the students who came passed the district's first math benchmark, against 38% of similar students who didn't.

I've mentored four student teachers from Columbia Plateau University, and the part of that work I like best is helping a new teacher see which lessons are working. I'd bring that to coaching your new math teachers in their classrooms.

I'd welcome a conversation about your curriculum adoption and the year ahead for your math teachers.
