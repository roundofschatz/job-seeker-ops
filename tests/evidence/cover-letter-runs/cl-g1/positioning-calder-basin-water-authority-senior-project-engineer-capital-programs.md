## 3. The hiring team's view

We came away thinking Graham gets the groundwork done before the work reaches the street. At Harlow Valley he started the lining program, wrote its specifications and ran a meeting for the residents before each street was lined, and his lining drew far fewer complaints per mile than open-trench work did. He runs his grant projects the same way, from a bid calendar that works back from each grant deadline. Our new lining group needs that habit, because we've hired out all of our lining until now, and whoever sets the group up will decide how we specify the work and how we treat the streets it runs through.

Our concern was how recent his lining work is. It comes from his years as a project engineer, and since he moved into his current job, his record has been about getting grant-funded projects to bid. What answered it was that Harlow Valley still uses the lining specifications he wrote, and his current job still covers sewer projects as well as water. He also lined more sewer main there than our first target.

We'd hire him to set up the lining group and lead its first projects, and we'd expect his first months to go into learning our sewer system and the contractors who've done our lining so far.

```text
Dear Calder Basin hiring team,

Calder Basin's new trenchless lining group needs someone to set it up and line its first ten miles of sewer main. Dana Ruiz in your engineering group is a former colleague of mine, and she said she'd pass my name along. I started Harlow Valley Utilities' trenchless sewer lining program in 2017, and the utility still uses the specifications I wrote for cured-in-place pipe lining.

At Harlow Valley I also ran the resident meetings before each street was lined. By 2020 the program had lined 14 miles of main at about 40% of the cost of open-trench replacement, and each mile of lined pipe drew about a quarter of the complaints that a mile of open-trench work did.

Each of Harlow Valley's state revolving fund grants came with a deadline, so I keep a bid calendar that works back from each one, and I meet the state's funding officer every month. From 2021 to 2025 I took nine of those projects to bid, $31 million in all. All nine reached bid on schedule, and the utility kept every dollar of its grant money. If you'd rather put me on your water main replacement program, I'd work its June 2027 deadline from the same kind of calendar, and keep one going for the projects in the ten-year, $600 million capital plan your board approved in May.

All of Calder Basin's sewer lining so far has been done by outside contractors. If I set up your new group, I'd begin with those contractors and the sewer mains they've lined for you. Then I'd write a lining specification for your mains, as I did at Harlow Valley, and put it into the bid packages for any lining you keep contracting out. Whether your own crews or a contractor does the work, I'd hold a resident meeting before each street is lined.

I've started a lining program and lined more than ten miles of main with it, and I can do the same for your new group. I'd like to talk about how the group should specify the lining for those first ten miles, and who should do it.

Best,
Graham Whitfield
```
