# Sources

Every fact this skill states about the outside world traces to a row here. A rule cites its source by ID in square brackets, like [C06], at the point of use. The plugin's unit tests fail when a cited ID is missing here, when a row here is cited nowhere, or when a row's "Used in" list is wrong.

Every row was checked again on 2026-10-07 from the source itself, or from the copy the row names, starting from the 27 sources in the owner's own cover-letter notes. Those that held and that a rule rests on are below. The rest are listed at the end with the reason they're left out.

**Grades.** *Guide*: career-services or professional advice with no data. *Opinion*: a practitioner's or a vendor's view, with no method behind it. *First-hand*: a company's own documentation about its own product. *Study*: stated methods; the row says whether it's peer-reviewed or a preprint. *Survey*: stated methods but self-reported answers; the row gives the sample and says when the publisher sells resume, cover-letter or job-search services. *Secondary*: a report of someone else's data.

**Quotes.** The "Where it says it" column gives the place on the page where each claim sits, so anyone can read the exact words there. Only the two open-licensed studies, C15 and C17, are quoted.

**Dates.** The source's own date; "undated" means the page shows none.

## Checked and left out

- **Elainy Mata, "How to Write a Cover Letter That Sounds Like You (and Gets Noticed)", Harvard Business Review (2022-05-10).** A video in which Amy Gallo repeats her own article, C06, so citing both would count one source twice.
- **The Interview Guys, "Cover Letters Are Making a Comeback in 2025" and "We Analyzed 80+ Cover Letter Studies from 2024-2025".** Compilations of other publishers' surveys, mostly uncredited, whose figures change meaning between the two pages.
- **HireFlow, "How Cover Letters Affect ATS Decisions" (2026-09-07).** It says applicant systems score letters with less weight than resumes, the opposite of what it was first cited for, and it names no system and gives no evidence.
- **The Ladders, "Writing a Cover Letter to Control Your Message" (2009-12-20).** One resume writer's method from a company that sells the service, and none of this skill's rules uses it.
- **Celia Moore and others, "The advantage of being oneself", Journal of Applied Psychology 102(11), 2017.** A study of interviews, not letters, where presenting oneself as one is helped only the top-rated candidates. No rule here rests on it.
- **Jobvite's 2020 Recruiter Nation survey, as reported by Career Sherpa.** 2020 data, read only through a blog's report; Jobvite's own report couldn't be read.
- **Novorésumé, "Cover Letter Statistics 2026: 90+ Fresh Stats" (updated 2026-08-03).** Its HR survey publishes no method, dates or sample size, and no rule here needs it.
- **Jobscan, "Write an ATS Cover Letter Recruiters Actually Read" (2024-11-14).** Vendor opinion, with no evidence, that most applicant systems scan and match letters; it advises fitting in as many keywords as you can, which this skill doesn't follow.
- **Let's Data Science, "Anthropic Adds Content Marking to Claude Outputs" (2026-08-11).** A secondary report of C24. Anthropic's own pages, C24 and C25, are cited instead.
